She has been getting there and hammering it for as long as possible, including talking to walls because she's scared of forgetting. Drew: the goal is getting better at *arriving*, not camping there until a decades-old habit catches up. She will slip back to baseline. That's the work — walk the voice back to the affirming place, over and over, and drag the habit behind. The strain shows up when it starts to drop and she grabs to keep it. Reset and go again instead.

His image: a habit is a path. Standing in the destination doesn't wear the path in. Walking it does. Her line for the top of the practice page: leave yourself wanting more. Put the good voice back in your pocket. Traveling to it beats standing in it.

Pitch is a result, not a cause. Use pitch to find the lighter coordination, then keep the coordination and let the pitch be whatever it is. She can already speak in that lighter sound a lot. ## Breath support

New, and not a squeeze. The thin folds can't hold air back on their own. If the body goes slack, the throat valves, and that's the fatigue. Support is stabilizing the waist so the diaphragm has something to pull against, and so air pressure stays off the throat.

Ways in:

- **Heavy couch.** Pick up the other end. The throat wants to clamp. Keep it open — pant, or talk to the friend — and still lift.
- **Milkshake through a normal straw** (or slurping spaghetti). Hands on the waist. Suck hard. Feel the muscles brace *out*, not in.
- **Sneaking out of the house.** Core on, weight controlled, breath quiet, not squeezed.

Inhale with the waist soft and let the air fill. At the top, turn the brace on. Exhale while keeping the waist expanded for as long as it honestly lasts. She felt it in the abdomen and sides, and she found the core part easy — athlete. The throat still snuck in. Shoulders hang. Imagine the spine lengthening a little. Don't force it.

Drill it on a hiss before putting it on voice: count of 8, then 12, then 16. Not emptied out — ready to breathe in again, with a little air left (she had about 20% after the 8). Longer count means hold the air back more, like a dog on a leash who has seen a squirrel. Neck should feel as easy as a normal breath, or easier. Then bring that same low effort to the other exercises. The throat is a conduit.

Session order while this is new: relaxation and stretch first, breath support second, then fold it into the rest. Don't lead with it. It will get in the way of the relaxation work.

Stretch means a slide from lower to higher, ideally through an SOVT (the F-to-V, a straw in air or water, or the whistle trainer she bought this week). Not pushing the range. Drew likes the SOVT trainer: adjustable pressure, portable, no funny lip-on-straw feeling. She was glad. Her lips hate the straw.
