## The motorbike

**Licensing: sorted.** Lexi holds a NZ **class 6** (motorcycle) licence and an **IDP** (confirmed 16 Nov 2026). That's the full legal requirement in Thailand; carry both originals on the bike, the IDP is only valid alongside the home licence.

Checkpoints are at both ends of Patong Hill, Chalong circle, Route 4233 Chalong–Rawai, Phuket Town, and the airport road. Being licensed means they're a 30-second wave-through rather than a fine, and more importantly it keeps travel insurance intact.

**What's left to do:**

- **Read the travel policy's motorbike clause.** Being licensed isn't the only condition: many policies cap engine size (some at 50cc, which rules out the standard 125cc scooter), and all of them require a helmet. If the cap is too low, buy a policy or add-on that covers 125cc before flying. Check for Thailand specifically.
- **Pick the bike.** A 125cc automatic (Honda Click / PCX, Yamaha NMAX) is the right tool: ~200–300 baht/day, cheaper by the week. The PCX/NMAX are more stable and comfortable for someone 6'2" on the hilly Kata–Karon–Patong and Nai Harn roads. Ask the villa to recommend a shop, or use a proper rental company with a written contract and clear damage terms.
- **Rental hygiene:** never leave your passport as deposit (photocopy + cash is normal), confirm the bike's compulsory insurance (Por Ror Bor) is current, ask in writing what the shop covers for damage, photograph every panel and scratch before riding off, and check brakes, tyres and lights before you leave the forecourt.
- **Helmet every ride, no exceptions.** Legally required for rider and pillion, fine up to 2,000 baht, and an unhelmeted crash is an insurance refusal. The shop's helmet will be rubbish; a 500–800 baht proper one from a Big C or motorbike shop is worth it for a week.
- **Riding notes:** left side like NZ. Scooters filter, cars don't look, sand on corners after rain. Patong Hill in both directions and the Kata–Karon hill are the two roads that hurt tourists; take them slow, especially the first time and after dark. Nights out: Grab, not the bike.

**Backup for days off the bike:** Grab and Bolt both run in Phuket, bike taxi or car, cheap by NZ standards. Useful for the Walking Street night, any evening involving a drink, and the airport.

## The "own holiday" structure

The thing that makes this work is a shape that is yours by default and joins the group by choice. Suggested frame, said out loud on night one so it doesn't read as avoidance:

> "I'm going to do my own thing most days. I'll be at dinner most nights and I'm keen for a couple of proper group days. Tell me which ones matter to you."

- **Mornings are yours, every day.** Up at 7, in the sea or on the bike before the heat and before anyone else is up. This is the single best hour of a Phuket day and nobody will fight you for it.
- **One anchor a day with the group**, usually dinner. Everything else optional.
- **Two or three genuinely shared things across the week**, chosen in advance: Loy Krathong on arrival night, one boat day, one long dinner. That's enough to be "on the family holiday."
- **One thing with Dad, one on one, low stakes, on your terms.** A sunrise coffee, a walk along a beach, a drive to a viewpoint. Don't wait for a Moment to happen in front of four men at a dinner table. Pick the time and place yourself, early in the week, so it isn't hanging over the rest of it.
- **Decide now how you'll handle a name or pronoun slip.** Light correction and carry on ("Lexi, Dad") is usually enough; or agree with one brother beforehand that he'll do it so you don't have to. Decide it before you're tired and sunburnt and it happens at dinner.
- **Exit plan is non-negotiable.** Whether it's the bike or the Grab app, you can leave any room at any time. Knowing that is most of what makes staying easy.
- **One thing Thailand gives you for free:** it is one of the most trans-visible countries on earth, marriage equality is law, and nobody in Phuket will blink at you. You will likely feel *more* ordinary walking down a street there than in Christchurch. Let that do some of the work.

## Chill picks (no temples)

**Beaches for doing nothing**

- **Nai Harn** – the locals' favourite in the south. Big, clean, calm in high season, lake behind it, good cafes. If the villa is anywhere south, this is your default morning.
- **Ya Nui** – tiny cove just past Nai Harn, best casual snorkelling on the island from the sand.
- **Ao Sane** – rocky, quiet, next door to Nai Harn, almost no one.
- **Kata Noi** – the quiet little sister of Kata. Good for a swim and a book.
- **Freedom Beach** – longtail from Patong or a steep walk down. Worth it once.
- **North (Bang Tao, Layan, Nai Thon, Banana Beach)** – if the villa is up north, these are where the space is. Nai Thon is the chillest.

**Food and wandering**

- **Phuket Old Town** – Sino-Portuguese shophouses, the best cafes on the island, proper southern Thai food. Go for a long, slow breakfast-into-lunch.
- **Sunday Walking Street (Lard Yai), Thalang Road, Old Town** – **Sunday 29 Nov**, roughly 4–10pm. Street food, music, local makers. The one "event" worth building a day around. Good as a group thing because you can wander off and find each other again.
- **Rawai seafood market** – pick your fish from the stalls, carry it across the road to be cooked. Cheap, excellent, zero pretension. A good group dinner.
- **Massage every day.** 300–400 baht for an hour of Thai massage at any clean-looking place. This is not a treat, it's the baseline.

**Move your body (the athlete option)**

- **Muay Thai drop-in class** – the Chalong/Rawai gym strip is the training capital of Thailand (Tiger Muay Thai is the famous one; there are a dozen others). Drop-in sessions welcome beginners, morning or late afternoon. A couple of hours of pads and bag work will clear your head better than anything else on this list.
- **Freediving / snorkel day** – Racha Yai or Coral Island from Chalong pier. Calm water, no temples, back by 4.
- **Sunrise swim at Nai Harn** – then coffee at the lake. Repeat.

**Day trips (the anti-Phi Phi)**

- **Koh Yao Noi** – longtail or speedboat from Bang Rong pier on the east coast (~30–45 min). Rubber plantations, empty roads, water buffalo, one main road, almost nothing to do. Rent a scooter there (or take yours across on the longtail; they'll load it), find a beach, eat at a hut. This is the single best "my own holiday" day on offer. Go alone or with one brother.
- **Phang Nga Bay by sea kayak** – the limestone karsts and sea caves without the James Bond Island bus-tour chaos. Small-group operators leave from the east coast.
- Skip Phi Phi day trips. Crowded, long boat, not chill.

**Sunset and evening**

- **Windmill Viewpoint** (above Nai Harn) – the view everyone goes to Promthep Cape for, minus the tour buses. 5:45pm.
- **Beach clubs** – if you want sunset with a soundtrack: Café del Mar (Kamala) and Catch (Bang Tao) are the known ones. Music is commercial house, not your music, but the sunset is real.
- **Patong, once, on your own terms** – the **Paradise Complex** off Rat-U-Thit Road is the gay quarter: bars, drag, kathoey shows, zero judgement, lots of fun. Bangla Road is the tourist meat market next door; you can look and leave. Simon Cabaret is the big kathoey show if you're curious; it's a tourist production and you may have mixed feelings about it.
- **Loy Krathong on night one** – buy a banana-leaf or bread krathong (not foam; sky lanterns are banned in Phuket), light the candle, float it. Nai Harn Lake if you're south, the Bang Tao lagoons if you're north, Saphan Hin for the full local festival. A lovely low-talk shared thing for five jet-lagged people.
