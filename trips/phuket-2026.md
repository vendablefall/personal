# Phuket, 24 November – 1 December 2026

*Drafted 16 November 2026. Suggestions, not bookings.*

## The facts

- **Who:** Lexi, Dad (Murray), brothers Gerard and Hugh, uncle Geoff (Mum's side). Five people, one villa.
- **When:** Tue 24 Nov → Tue 1 Dec 2026. Seven nights.
- **Where:** a villa in **Rawai**, the southern tip of the island, a 23-minute walk from Nai Harn beach. Quiet, expat-heavy, cafe-rich, and the far end of the island from Patong. Nai Harn, Ya Nui, Ao Sane, Promthep and the Windmill Viewpoint are all 5–10 minutes on the bike; Chalong (pier, gym strip) ~10–15 min; Kata ~15 min; Old Town ~30 min; Patong ~40 min; airport ~1h15.
- **Arrival:** **Geoff is picking Lexi up from the airport.** It's a long drive south (~1h15), a good hour of one-on-one with the uncle before the full house.
- **What she wants:** chill and relaxed. No temples (seen them all). A motorbike. Her *own* holiday inside the group one, not beholden to the group.
- **Why it's loaded:** first real time with Dad since being Lexi; first overseas trip as Lexi that isn't a surgery trip.
- **Conditions:** last week of November is the start of high season. Showers mostly gone, west-coast sea flattening out, boat trips reliable, prices not yet at December peak. Days ~31°C, nights ~25°C, sea ~29°C. Sunset ~6:05pm.
- **Landing-night bonus:** **Tuesday 24 Nov is Loy Krathong**, the full-moon festival where everyone floats a candle-lit krathong on the water after dark. From Rawai the obvious spot is **Nai Harn Lake** (a family favourite with locals, calm, right behind the beach, walkable from the villa) or **Rawai pier** itself, where the longtails are. Saphan Hin in Phuket Town is the full local festival (16–25 Nov) if anyone wants the big version; Patong is the loud one. Peak is ~8–10pm.

## The motorbike

**Licensing: sorted.** Lexi holds a NZ **class 6** (motorcycle) licence and an **IDP** (confirmed 16 Nov 2026). That's the full legal requirement in Thailand; carry both originals on the bike, the IDP is only valid alongside the home licence.

Checkpoints are at both ends of Patong Hill, Chalong circle, Route 4233 Chalong–Rawai, Phuket Town, and the airport road. Being licensed means they're a 30-second wave-through rather than a fine, and more importantly it keeps travel insurance intact.

**What's left to do:**

- **Read the travel policy's motorbike clause.** Being licensed isn't the only condition: many policies cap engine size (some at 50cc, which rules out the standard 125cc scooter), and all of them require a helmet. If the cap is too low, buy a policy or add-on that covers 125cc before flying. Check for Thailand specifically.
- **Pick the bike.** A 125cc automatic (Honda Click / PCX, Yamaha NMAX) is the right tool: ~200–300 baht/day, cheaper by the week. The PCX/NMAX are more stable and comfortable for someone 6'2" on the hilly Kata–Karon–Patong and Nai Harn roads. Rawai is full of rental shops along Wiset Road and around the pier; ask the villa to recommend one, or use a proper rental company with a written contract and clear damage terms. Many will deliver to the villa.
- **Rental hygiene:** never leave your passport as deposit (photocopy + cash is normal), confirm the bike's compulsory insurance (Por Ror Bor) is current, ask in writing what the shop covers for damage, photograph every panel and scratch before riding off, and check brakes, tyres and lights before you leave the forecourt.
- **Helmet every ride, no exceptions.** Legally required for rider and pillion, fine up to 2,000 baht, and an unhelmeted crash is an insurance refusal. The shop's helmet will be rubbish; a 500–800 baht proper one from a Big C or motorbike shop is worth it for a week.
- **Riding notes:** left side like NZ. Scooters filter, cars don't look, sand on corners after rain. Patong Hill in both directions and the Kata–Karon hill are the two roads that hurt tourists; take them slow, especially the first time and after dark. Nights out: Grab, not the bike.

**Backup for days off the bike:** Grab and Bolt both run in Phuket, bike taxi or car, cheap by NZ standards. Useful for the Walking Street night, any evening involving a drink, and the airport.

## The "own holiday" structure

The thing that makes this work is a shape that is yours by default and joins the group by choice. Suggested frame, said out loud on night one so it doesn't read as avoidance:

> "I'm going to do my own thing most days. I'll be at dinner most nights and I'm keen for a couple of proper group days. Tell me which ones matter to you."

- **Mornings are yours, every day.** Up at 7, in the sea or on the bike before the heat and before anyone else is up. This is the single best hour of a Phuket day and nobody will fight you for it.
- **One anchor a day with the group**, usually dinner. Everything else optional.
- **Two or three genuinely shared things across the week**, chosen in advance: Loy Krathong on arrival night, one boat day, one long dinner. That's enough to be "on the family holiday."
- **One thing with Dad, one on one, low stakes, on your terms.** A sunrise coffee, a walk along a beach, a drive to a viewpoint. Don't wait for a Moment to happen in front of four men at a dinner table. Pick the time and place yourself, early in the week, so it isn't hanging over the rest of it.
- **Decide now how you'll handle a name or pronoun slip.** Light correction and carry on ("Lexi, Dad") is usually enough; or agree with one brother beforehand that he'll do it so you don't have to. Decide it before you're tired and sunburnt and it happens at dinner.
- **Exit plan is non-negotiable.** Whether it's the bike or the Grab app, you can leave any room at any time. Knowing that is most of what makes staying easy.
- **One thing Thailand gives you for free:** it is one of the most trans-visible countries on earth, marriage equality is law, and nobody in Phuket will blink at you. You will likely feel *more* ordinary walking down a street there than in Christchurch. Let that do some of the work.

## Chill picks (no temples)

**Beaches for doing nothing**

- **Nai Harn** – the locals' favourite in the south and your default morning: 23 min walk or 5 on the bike. Big, clean, calm in high season, lake behind it, good cafes. Sunrise swim, then coffee.
- **Ya Nui** – tiny cove 5 min past Nai Harn towards Promthep, best casual snorkelling on the island straight off the sand. Kayak and snorkel hire on the beach.
- **Ao Sane** – rocky, quiet, through the Nai Harn hotel road, almost no one.
- **Laem Ka** – small, sheltered beach on the Rawai side, east-facing, barely on the map. Calm when the west coast isn't.
- **Rawai Beach itself** isn't for swimming (longtail pier, mudflat at low tide). It's for the seafood market, the sunset over the boats, and hiring a longtail.
- **Kata Noi** – 15 min north, the quiet little sister of Kata. Good for a swim and a book.
- **Freedom Beach** – longtail from Patong or a steep walk down. Worth it once if you're over that side anyway.

**Food and wandering**

- **Phuket Old Town** – Sino-Portuguese shophouses, the best cafes on the island, proper southern Thai food. Go for a long, slow breakfast-into-lunch.
- **Sunday Walking Street (Lard Yai), Thalang Road, Old Town** – **Sunday 29 Nov**, roughly 4–10pm. Street food, music, local makers. The one "event" worth building a day around. Good as a group thing because you can wander off and find each other again.
- **Rawai seafood market** – walking distance from the villa. Pick your fish, prawns and crab from the stalls, carry them across the road to one of the restaurants to be cooked. Cheap, excellent, zero pretension. The obvious group dinner.
- **Rawai / Nai Harn cafes** – Rawai's expat population means genuinely good coffee, brunch and healthy places on every corner between the villa and the beach. You won't need to leave the neighbourhood for a good breakfast all week.
- **Massage every day.** 300–400 baht for an hour of Thai massage at any clean-looking place. This is not a treat, it's the baseline.

**Move your body (nothing combat, nothing competitive)**

*Rawai is the Muay Thai capital of Thailand and the gym signs will be everywhere. Not for Lexi: no Muay Thai, no combat anything. Her kind of movement here is water, breath and stretch.*

- **Yoga** – Rawai and Nai Harn have several drop-in studios (the expat scene keeps them busy); morning classes before the heat. Look for a sunrise or beachside class.
- **Kayak or SUP at Ya Nui** – hire on the beach, paddle round the headland to Nai Harn or out to the little island off the cove. Flat water in high season.
- **Snorkelling straight off the sand** at Ya Nui and Ao Sane. No boat, no group, no schedule.
- **Freediving / snorkel day** – Racha Yai or Coral Island from Chalong pier, 15 min away. Calm water, no temples, back by 4. Several freediving schools are based in Rawai/Chalong if you want a proper session.
- **Sunrise swim at Nai Harn** – then coffee at the lake. Repeat.

**Day trips (the anti-Phi Phi)**

- **Koh Bon** – the Rawai local secret. A 10–15 minute longtail from Rawai pier to a small island with one beach restaurant, a reef, and nothing else. Hire a longtail at the pier (they'll quote a return fare and come back for you at an agreed time) or go with the restaurant's own boat. The easiest "my own holiday" day there is: out by 10, back by 4, no planning.
- **Koh Lon** – the bigger, even quieter island next to Koh Bon, also from Rawai pier. Fishing village, mangroves, a couple of beaches.
- **Koh Yao Noi** – the proper away-day. From Rawai it's ~1h10 on the bike to Bang Rong pier on the north-east coast, then a 30–45 min longtail or speedboat. Rubber plantations, empty roads, water buffalo, one main road, almost nothing to do. Rent a scooter there (or take yours across on the longtail), find a beach, eat at a hut. A long day from Rawai, so it's the one to do if you want to be genuinely gone all day, alone or with one brother.
- **Phang Nga Bay by sea kayak** – the limestone karsts and sea caves without the James Bond Island bus-tour chaos. Small-group operators leave from the east coast; most will collect from Rawai.
- Skip Phi Phi day trips. Crowded, long boat, not chill.

**Sunset and evening**

- **Windmill Viewpoint** (above Nai Harn, 5 min from the villa) – the view everyone goes to Promthep Cape for, minus the tour buses. 5:45pm. Promthep itself is 2 min further if someone in the group wants the famous one; go once, early in the week, then use the windmill.
- **Rawai pier at dusk** – longtails, the seafood market lighting up, locals eating on the seawall. The low-effort nightly option.
- **Beach clubs** – if you want sunset with a soundtrack: Café del Mar (Kamala) and Catch (Bang Tao) are the known ones. Music is commercial house, not your music, but the sunset is real.
- **Patong, once, on your own terms** – the **Paradise Complex** off Rat-U-Thit Road is the gay quarter: bars, drag, kathoey shows, zero judgement, lots of fun. Bangla Road is the tourist meat market next door; you can look and leave. Simon Cabaret is the big kathoey show if you're curious; it's a tourist production and you may have mixed feelings about it.
- **Loy Krathong on night one** – buy a banana-leaf or bread krathong (not foam; sky lanterns are banned in Phuket), light the candle, float it. From the villa: Nai Harn Lake (walkable, calm, local families) or Rawai pier. A lovely low-talk shared thing for five jet-lagged people, and it needs no bike and no planning.
