# 26 November 2026

*Written from Thailand, ~7am local.* She's on a family trip with her two brothers and her uncle — and she's **meeting her dad for the first time as Lexi.** Nearly five years after coming out. More below once it's happened; recording it now so the date is on the record.

Two other things, both about women she's close to, both landing in the same week.

## Slut-shamed by cis women

**The shift.** Since the facelift and second nose job (May 2026) the body has changed a lot: lost a lot of upper-body muscle, put weight on in the lower body, new face. In her words, "many points hotter, if that's a point system." And the reaction from cis women around her has shifted with it. Two women in particular have started calling her a slut, a whore, "hypersexualized" — the kind of thing that used to never be said to her face.

**The gaslight.** When she raises it with her cis friends, she gets "oh, I know — this is such a supportive environment." So she's being called a whore in a room that insists it's supportive. Her words: "I feel gaslit both ways." She's not sure if it's projection, if they're intimidated, or if they're applying adult cis-woman rules to a trans woman who came out four years ago and has only just arrived at this level of conventional attractiveness ("patriarchal attraction, let's call it"). She notes the two women in question are themselves women who use their sexuality to get their way with men.

**Her questions:** Is this common? Am I being gaslit? Do I bail on them?

**The answers she got, kept here so they're on record:**

- Yes, it's common. Slut-shaming is the standard tool women use on women they've reclassified as competition. Trans women who become conventionally attractive describe this almost universally: for the first couple of years you're the brave friend and the ally badge; cross a line of attractiveness and the frame flips to rival.
- Her adolescence point is right and is the most important thing she said. She is ~4 years into womanhood with a 39-year-old's body, money and agency. Cis women did their experimental, hypersexual phase at 17–23, got slut-shamed by their peers then, and have mostly forgotten they did it. She's doing hers now and being graded against the rules for a settled 39-year-old.
- "Hypersexualized" out of a cis woman's mouth, aimed at a trans woman, is the hundred-year-old transmisogynist trope wearing feminism's clothes.
- "They're intimidated by me" may be partly true but it's unfalsifiable and feels good, so don't lean on it. Why doesn't matter. What they do when told to stop is the only real test.
- Honest note: if either woman has a partner or a crush who's been noticing Lexi, that is almost certainly where the heat is. Not her fault; just the trigger for this exact behaviour.

**The plan:** say it once, privately, flat, to each of them: "When you call me a slut or a whore, I don't hear it as a joke. Stop." Then watch. A friend apologises and stops; a competitor says "can't you take a joke" or does it again in front of people. With the "supportive environment" bystanders: stop asking them to agree it's happening, tell them what's needed — "say something when it happens." If the two don't adjust, quietly reduce how much of her they get. No blow-up. Put the time into the women who've actually shown up.

> She spent a quarter of a million dollars and six surgeries across three countries to be exactly the woman who's getting called a slut right now. Two people losing a competition she didn't enter don't get to shrink her.

## Shaz — the road trip that quietly stopped being a plan

**What happened.** At the movie night they talked summer plans. In Lexi's head, the last time they'd properly talked, they were doing a road trip together. At the movie night it became apparent Shaz had made other plans — with a friend or friends she didn't name — and didn't want to line up times so they'd overlap down south. Shaz also said something like "oh, we're in Wanaka" as if that were separate from Lexi, apparently not having registered that Lexi's bus is in Wanaka and that Wanaka is where Lexi's whole down-south life is, every time she goes.

**How it landed.** In that moment she didn't feel like a friend. She felt like an acquaintance Shaz was trying to dodge. "The bad smell friend — the one you make plans with when you've got nothing, and then when something else comes along it's 'oh yeah, no, I'm doing something else now, don't worry.'" She felt like a loser being brushed off, and she doesn't want to feel that way about her friends — she'd rather just not.

**What she did.** Sent Shaz a long voice-dictated Messenger message (today, ~7am where she is), opening with "make sure you have spoons for this, this means a lot to me." Says repeatedly that it's okay if plans changed and okay if Shaz doesn't want to hang out — but that Shaz could have *told* her. Says the thing that sits with her isn't the plans, it's that Shaz didn't seem to want her to know. Closes, three times, with "I'd love to hear your side of this." Awaiting a reply.

**Status:** "Shaz and my relationship isn't going so well." First recorded strain in a friendship that's otherwise been one of the two load-bearing ones in Wellington (see `about-me.md`).

## Dad, Thailand

The promise at eleven, in her own words from `early-years.md`: *"if you ever make it, I'm going to change myself and run away. Run away from my family, run away from my friends, and start a new life as the real me."* She made it. She changed herself. And then, instead of running away from her family, she got on a plane to meet her father as the real her. Whatever happens in the room, that is the fact of this week.

## The thread underneath the other two

Same nerve, twice in a week: *am I someone people actually choose, or someone they settle for when nothing better is on?* The two cis women are recasting her as a threat; Shaz (as it felt) recast her as optional. Jack in October was the same shape — promised the next door, didn't walk through it. Worth watching that she doesn't fold three different situations into one verdict about herself. The slut-shaming is about those two women. The Shaz thing is one conversation at a movie night, not yet a verdict, and Shaz hasn't replied yet.
