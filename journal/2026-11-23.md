# 23 November 2026

**Traeh, and what was underneath it.** A Monday afternoon message exchange that turned out to be about something much bigger — and the first time the festival assault and the rumour have gone on record here.

## The Traeh exchange

Over the weekend Lexi felt an energy shift from Traeh Song. She sent a long pre-emptive apology: didn't know what she'd done, sorry if she'd hurt anyone, "still learning how to be in the world," didn't need a reply, and — the line that mattered — *"i wont say hi in the future to save myself the embarrassment."* Two sorrys in one message.

Traeh's reply, 4:30pm: *"I'm not sure what you're referring to, but I'm not holding any ill will. I was working & quite overwhelmed at times. I honestly don't remember crossing paths, but sorry if you felt slighted."*

She wanted to apologise a third time. Instead she sent:

> oh my gosh, okay — that is such a relief. i think i read way too much into a busy weekend and spun myself out over it. thank you for being so kind about it. and please ignore the dramatic "won't say hi" bit, i will absolutely say hi 😅 hope you get some rest x

Thank you instead of sorry; walked back the self-exile line; kept it short. Done well.

## What it was actually about

Her own words, straight after: *"i think this is linked to the voices i heard at resonance, saying i was a 'bad cow' and that i 'lied about transphobia to get attention' which i didn't do."*

Then she pasted a context summary she'd written for another AI, from Korea, around May/June 2026. The facts in it, as she gave them:

- **The attack.** At a festival early in 2026 — her summary says **Splore**, today she said **Resonance** (20–23 Mar, North Head); *which one is unconfirmed* — she was very intoxicated and was targeted by a group of guys and the girls with them, who mocked her for being trans. Shoved from behind, hit her head, passed out, **stopped breathing, and needed Narcan.** Sober since. (This is the real origin of the "no drugs, home by 1–2am" shift that was already on file as a point of pride.)
- **The aftermath.** A day or two later she texted her flatmates she'd been attacked and was coming home — then didn't, and didn't follow up. Stayed up north two weeks with two groups of close friends: medical checkups, therapy three times a week. Couldn't face the 10-hour drive alone, didn't want to leave the van. Then Korea for the facelift/nose surgery, home briefly, back to Korea for a complication revision. Lots of therapy and **EMDR** since; she describes herself as in a good place with it. Told only her flatmates, the police, her therapist, and the people she was with.
- **The overheard line.** Once, outside her van, someone she couldn't see said something like *"fake transphobia for sympathy."* That is the one piece of the rumour she has directly witnessed.
- **The theory.** She suspects a flatmate (recently no longer a friend) passed her story to a toxic almost-ex in the community — someone many people know to exaggerate and vilify, whom Lexi cut off after seeing how mean she could be — who is spreading that Lexi faked the transphobia for sympathy. She feels many people have distanced.
- **What she did.** Chose not to confront. From Korea, wrote a calm, accountable essay to the flat group chat: what happened, why she went quiet ("survival mode"), an apology for not following up, the therapy/EMDR, a boundary that it's a sensitive memory. The flatmate in question read it and said nothing. Only Sarah, the newest flatmate, replied warmly. Nobody else reacted.
- **The best friend.** A few days later, her best friend in that circle (not a flatmate, never told by her) messaged: hasn't been a good friend, ages since he's seen her, wants to catch up. He must have heard secondhand — likely via his sister, who is best friends with the almost-ex. His replies had gone short and unlike him. Her plan at the time: go, stay calm and unperformative, tell it plainly if it comes up, ask gently how he heard, and if he's an "envoy": *"I know what happened to me, I've got medical records, I've done the work in therapy, I'm not here to defend myself to people who weren't there, but I'm happy to talk to anyone who comes to me directly."* Keep a quiet record of anything concrete. Don't chase people who've distanced.

**Outcome of that catch-up: not yet on record.** The summary is six months old; what's happened since with him, the flatmate, and the almost-ex is an open question.

## The honest read

She asked for objective input and to be told where she might be over-reading. So:

**Confirmed:** the attack (police, medical records, witnesses, Narcan). One overheard sentence, speaker unseen. Flat-chat silence except Sarah. The best friend's message.

**Inferred:** the pipeline flatmate → almost-ex → rumour → "many people have distanced" → envoy. Each link is plausible. None is confirmed, and each leans on the one before it.

**The simpler explanation for the distancing** needs no rumour at all: she vanished for roughly three months; she went sober in a scene whose friendships mostly live at parties; she then sent a heavy, important message into a group chat from overseas. Group-chat silence after a trauma disclosure is close to universal — people freeze, don't know what's appropriate in front of six others, assume someone else will answer. Sarah replying is what one brave person does; the rest going quiet is what most people do. It reads as disbelief when you're braced for disbelief. It usually isn't.

**Today was a test.** She picked one of the "distanced" people and asked directly. Result: no ill will, overwhelmed, doesn't remember crossing paths. Not proof the rumour doesn't exist — the van line is real — but hard evidence that the radius is smaller than the model in her head. The model says *everyone has heard and believes it*. The evidence says *one person said something once, and at least one person I'd written off had no idea*.

**The real danger** isn't the rumour. It's self-exile. "I won't say hi to save myself the embarrassment" makes the distance real whether or not anyone is spreading anything — and if the almost-ex is doing this, withdrawal hands her the result for free. The antidote is what she did today: one short, direct check, answer taken at face value.

**On the "voices."** Memories from the night of the attack — very intoxicated, head injury, unconscious, Narcan — are genuinely unreliable, through no fault of hers. "Bad cow," if it's from that night, should not carry the same weight as the van moment, which she heard sober. The attack itself is not in question. Which sentences were said, by whom, during it, is.
