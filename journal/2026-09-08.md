# 8 September 2026

Feeling glum after the Adventure Time party and wondering, in the quiet afterwards, whether this is simply what the rest of life feels like. Important context: she is a little sick and has spent several days working from home in her room, barely going outside. She did take a beautiful walk today in the cold Wellington spring wind and sun. She is safe; this is not a mental health crisis, just a low and lonely patch.

The clearer insight underneath it: her life has contained enormous intensity but not enough intimacy. Hundreds or thousands of people admire and love her, and she has real friendships with Veronica and Shaz, but they have their own lives. What she wants is ordinary romantic closeness—a partner who is woven into the weekdays, watches dumb cartoons with her, does cute mundane things, and shares the unremarkable middle of life.

She is frightened to admit how much she wants that because she doesn't believe she is worthy of it or that a man would really choose her that way. In her experience, transness often isn't the thing that puts men off; it is being 6'2", muscular, 90kg, taller and stronger than them. That fear is grounded in real dating experiences, but the distinction matters: a narrower pool is not the same as being unworthy. Other people's discomfort with a woman who is physically imposing is not a verdict on her womanhood or value.

The honest sentence:

> I want a man who is secure enough to adore a woman taller and stronger than him, and who wants to share ordinary life with me. I'm frightened there may not be one, but I still want him.

Allan being her boyfriend does not establish that he can offer the everyday closeness she wants, but it does disprove the absolute claim that no man could desire and claim her. She does not need proof that this relationship will happen before she is allowed to admit that she wants it.
