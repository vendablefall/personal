# Early years — NZ, school, and the rugby ascent (1996–2010)

As told by Lexi, 4 Sep 2026. Names/years approximate where noted.

## Wanaka (early 1996 – ~late 1996)

Landed from Saudi Arabia — average temperature 30–40° — into a Wanaka spring. Freezing: two jackets and mittens while everyone else was fine, which added to being the different kid and the teasing. Went to Wanaka Primary. Only there ~9 months: her dad is a **CFO**, and there was no CFO work in Wanaka.

## Christchurch (1997 – 2010)

**Fendalton Primary (a few months, ~1997).** Bullied relentlessly — kids would wait outside the school to kick her. "A soft, queer little boy before everyone really knew what queer was." Fendalton being the fancy area, "with all the bigotry and hatred that comes with white Christchurch."

**Ilam Primary.** Her mum pulled her out of Fendalton because the bullying was so bad and moved her to Ilam — attached to the University of Canterbury, full of lecturers' kids, a much better demographic for a different kid. Much better. In her last year at Ilam the sport started to click: friends and sporting acumen arrived together.

**Cobham Intermediate (2 years).** Living in Fendalton at the time, in a big old mansion. This is where the rugby really took off — made the top teams, started being the cool kid at school. The lesson that would run her life for the next fifteen years was being installed: being good at rugby = being safe and valued.

**Burnside High, 3rd form (~2001) — the gummy bear year.** Puberty kicked in and she had a huge freak-out she only understood decades later: she didn't want her body to change into a man's. At the time she had no idea what it was — just that she couldn't be there. She bunked school and sat in the mall eating gummy bears for eight hours a day rather than going to school and being a boy. *"Yuck. I'd rather be absent."* Failed 3rd form. Labelled a bad kid.

**Christchurch Boys' High (~2002–2003, 4th & 5th form).** Her parents sent her to Boys' High — a serious rugby school — to "straighten her out." They put her in the lowest class for 4th form; she came 3rd in class, because she was never a bad student — she was having a gender meltdown. Two years later she was in the top English, maths and science classes across the board — and **hid her report card from her rugby friends** because they hassled her for being a nerd. None of her rugby mates were in any of her classes. (The double life — excellence in two worlds, visible in neither — starts here, years before Brisbane.)

Rugby union accelerated hard at Boys' High: 15As and 16As, the best teams in Christchurch — won their divisions.

**Back to Burnside (~2004–2005, last two years).** After two years of succeeding at Boys' High, she was given the choice of school — and left for Burnside, the worse rugby school, because she hated Boys' High. ("My rugby career probably would have been better if I'd stayed. But I'm trans, and I hated that place.") Played 1st XV at Burnside, a division down; lost to the Boys' High 1st XV by plenty, "which was shameful."

## Club rugby → the code switch (~2006–2010)

**Christchurch Football Club (union), Colts, 3 years.** First year: Colts Black (the B team) — won the championship. Then two years in Colts A: top three all three years — 1st, 2nd, 3rd, in that order.

**The Spain tour (~2008).** Toured with the CFC seniors — played the Spanish national team and several clubs. "We spanked them all." Three weeks in Madrid. When she got back, they dropped her from the top team — so she quit union and switched to rugby league.

**Papanui Tigers (league, ~2008–2010).** Turned out she was a much better league player than union player.
- Year 1: made the Christchurch/Canterbury **Wests representative team** (best of the West).
- Year 2 (~2009–10): made the **New Zealand Residents rugby league team**, plus Wests and **Canterbury** rep teams. Toured all over New Zealand.

Then 2011: Brisbane, between the earthquakes, and the Queensland Cup. (See `timeline.md` and `career.md`.)

## Threads worth noticing

- The bullying didn't stop because she changed — it stopped when sport made her valuable. That's the wiring: worth = performance.
- The gummy bear year is the earliest documented signal of gender dysphoria — a body refusing the future being forced on it, fifteen years before she had the words.
- The hidden report card at Boys' High is the double life in miniature: too smart for the rugby boys, too rugby for anyone to see the rest. She'd run that exact play again in Brisbane (pro footy + full-time IT).
- She chose escape from Boys' High over a better rugby career — even at 16, given one free choice, she picked away from the mask. Worth remembering when she wonders whether she "always chose performance."
