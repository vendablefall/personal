# Germany work visa research

*Researched 8 September 2026. Immigration thresholds change annually, so recheck before applying. This is practical research, not legal advice.*

## Bottom line

Lexi's prospects for obtaining German work permission are **very strong once she has a qualifying German job offer**. The visa is unlikely to be the difficult part; finding the right role, location and compensation package is the meaningful hurdle.

Her most obvious route is the **EU Blue Card for experienced IT professionals without an IT degree**. Germany explicitly permits this route for IT specialists who can demonstrate at least three years of university-level IT experience during the previous seven years. Lexi has approximately 15 years in enterprise IT, including eight years designing hyperscale cloud systems and four years as a Senior Software Architect.

The practical assessment:

- **Eligibility after receiving a suitable offer: very high**
- **Likelihood of finding a qualifying role: good, but not automatic**
- **Overall visa risk: low**
- **Main risks:** securing an English-speaking senior role in the desired city, German hiring conditions in 2027, bureaucracy and processing time—not a lack of qualifications.

## Best visa route: EU Blue Card for IT specialists

Official 2026 requirements relevant to Lexi:

1. A specific German job offer or employment contract as an IT professional.
2. Employment lasting at least six months.
3. At least three of the previous seven years spent working in IT at an academic/professional level.
4. Gross annual salary of at least **€45,934.20 in 2026** for the IT-specialist route.
5. Approval from the Federal Employment Agency where required.

The threshold is revised annually, so the 2027 figure must be checked when applying.

Senior cloud architecture roles should normally clear this threshold comfortably. Current advertised examples include senior/lead cloud roles around €90,000–€130,000, although individual advertisements and recruiter salary guides are not guarantees of the broader market.

Official source: [EU Blue Card — Make it in Germany](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/eu-blue-card)

Official IT overview: [Visa options for IT professionals](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/it-professionals)

## Alternative route

Germany also has a visa for professionally experienced IT workers. The 2026 guidance allows IT professionals without a formal IT qualification to qualify with:

- at least two years of relevant IT experience during the previous five years;
- a concrete German job offer; and
- salary of at least **€45,630 in 2026**, subject to Federal Employment Agency approval, unless collective-agreement provisions apply.

Lexi easily meets the experience requirement. The Blue Card is probably preferable because of its mobility and faster path to permanent residence, but this route provides a useful fallback.

Official source: [IT specialists and managers from third countries](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/fileadmin/1_Rebrush_2022/a_Fachkraefte/PDF-Dateien/3_Visum_u_Aufenthalt/Visagrafik_EN/Visaoptionen_IT_aus_Drittstaaten_EN.pdf)

## New Zealand passport advantage

New Zealand citizens are privileged under German immigration rules:

- Lexi may enter Germany without obtaining an entry visa first, including when intending to seek a residence title for employment.
- She must register her German address and obtain the appropriate residence permit **before beginning work**.
- The residence-permit application must be made within the permitted entry period.
- If she wants to start work immediately after arrival, applying for the work visa from New Zealand before travelling is safer than waiting for a Berlin immigration appointment.

Official source: [How to apply for a visa — Make it in Germany](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/apply-for-visa)

## The employment market

Germany's general IT market weakened during 2025–26:

- The Federal Employment Agency reported that broad software-development shortages were no longer evident in 2025.
- Average reported IT vacancies fell, and unemployment in IT rose.
- However, demand increasingly favours specialists and highly qualified experts. More than 40% of new IT vacancies were directed at expert-level candidates, with another third aimed at specialists.

That distinction works in Lexi's favour. She is not competing as a generic developer. Multi-cloud platform architecture, petabyte-scale geospatial data, security/compliance, Kubernetes, Terraform and AI governance place her in the expert segment the market continues to value.

Official source: [German Federal Employment Agency — ICT labour market](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/presse/2026-24-arbeitsmarkt-in-der-informations-und-kommunikationstechnik-ikt-im-spannungsfeld-konjunkturelle-schwaeche-trifft-auf-strukturellen-wandel)

## Berlin-specific reality

Berlin has English-speaking startup, scale-up and international-company roles, particularly in cloud-native infrastructure, data platforms, security and AI. German fluency is not universally required at this level, but lacking German will narrow the market, especially among established local companies and public-sector work.

The biggest mismatch may be location rather than visa eligibility. Trimble has relevant engineering operations and current cloud/data roles in Germany, but examples found were based in **Stuttgart and Munich**, often with substantial office attendance. An internal Trimble transfer could be administratively easier than an external hire, but it may not produce a Berlin-based position.

The strongest employment approaches would be:

1. Ask Trimble about a transfer onto German payroll while retaining or adapting the current global platform role.
2. Search for Principal Cloud Architect, Platform Architect, Staff/Principal Platform Engineer, Cloud Security Architect, Infrastructure Architect and Engineering Manager roles.
3. Target international employers where English is the operating language.
4. Position FedRAMP as evidence of designing within stringent regulated environments, while foregrounding GDPR and ISO 27001 for European relevance.
5. Lead with scale and organisational leverage: eight regions, petabyte-scale data and platform patterns used by 40 engineers.
6. Begin German lessons. Even A1/A2 would improve daily life and demonstrate commitment; B1 has a direct immigration benefit.

## Permanent-residence potential

The Blue Card can provide a relatively fast route to permanent settlement:

- after **27 months** of qualifying employment with A1 German; or
- after **21 months** with B1 German,

subject to pension contributions and the other settlement requirements.

Official source: [Settlement permit — Make it in Germany](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/living-permanently/settlement-permit)
